# Portfolio checklist

What a product-UX portfolio is judged on, distilled from hiring-manager and
portfolio-review sources (researched 2026-10-14; sources at the bottom). Use it
as the fixed bar for audits: every item has an ID so findings can cite it.

Most of this is practitioner consensus, not controlled research. Treat any
"reviewers spend N seconds" figure as anecdotal; the point is that the first
pass is a skim.

## H — Homepage & positioning

- **H1** A specific role title and a one-line value proposition above the fold.
  A reviewer should know what you do and why it matters without scrolling.
- **H2** The hero's claim is one the work below can prove. Case-study titles
  echo it.
- **H3** Every card says what you did, who it was for, and a hook (the most
  impressive true fact), plus role/industry labels for sorting.
- **H4** Card titles name an outcome or problem, not just a product name.
- **H5** Hover/motion never hides the work; no cursor-followers or
  scroll-jacking. Usable beats flashy.
- **H6** Social proof where it exists: labeled testimonials (who said it),
  recognizable logos, talks/writing.
- **H7** The next step is effortless: a clear contact button at the end, plus a
  copy-email option (mailto often opens the wrong app).
- **H8** Header nav carries Work · About · Résumé · Contact. Anything else is
  secondary.

## C — Case studies

- **C1** Opening block (3–5 lines): product, problem/constraint, your role,
  team, timeline, one-line outcome.
- **C2** Structure: Problem → Process (short, under ~200 words) → Decisions →
  Outcomes → Reflection.
- **C3** Decisions name the rejected option and why: one "what I didn't do"
  sentence per major choice. This is the section that earns callbacks.
- **C4** Headings state findings, so the headings alone tell the story.
- **C5** ~1,000–1,500 words / about a 5-minute read; visuals carry most of each
  section (roughly 70/30).
- **C6** Every visual has a caption or annotation. Context-free visuals are
  decoration.
- **C7** Outcomes are measured, or honestly marked as not measured. Proxies
  (task completion, usability findings) beat vague praise.
- **C8** Interactive prototypes/demos sit early, not at the end.
- **C9** No abrupt ending: learnings, then a link onward to the next study.
- **C10** Not a textbook bootcamp sequence (empathize → define → ideate…).
  Real, messy iteration is more credible.
- **C11** Ownership is explicit: what you did vs. what the team did.
- **C12** Cold-read test: in 60 seconds a stranger can say what you decided and
  what you decided *not* to do.

## T — Truthfulness, NDA & AI

- **T1** At least one real, shipped project. Concept work is fine but weaker
  evidence, and must be visibly labeled.
- **T2** NDA work: keep industry, role, team scope, process and generalized
  artifacts; drop names, branding, real screens and metrics; say it's under NDA.
  Avoid password walls (or give the password in the application).
- **T3** No figure a reader could mistake for a claimed result unless it's
  real, including copy inside mockups.
- **T4** AI use is described: where it contributed and where judgment was
  yours. Never hidden, never a gimmick.
- **T5** Everything on the site is consistent with the résumé and LinkedIn.

## A — About, résumé & contact

- **A1** About: 2–3 paragraphs on approach and background, plus a photo. A few
  human details. Not a life story.
- **A2** Résumé PDF linked from nav, About and footer; current and matching the
  site.
- **A3** Contact: email plus a working form; clear success and error states.
- **A4** Nothing on the page dilutes the target role (off-target certs,
  unrelated links) without a reason to be there.

## Q — The site as a work sample

- **Q1** Works on phones: no horizontal scroll, readable type, ≥24px targets.
- **Q2** Fast: good Core Web Vitals, optimized images, no layout shift.
- **Q3** WCAG 2.2 AA: contrast, keyboard, focus, reduced motion, alt text.
- **Q4** HTTPS, no broken links (especially card → case study), a helpful 404.
- **Q5** Metadata: title, description, OG image per page, so shared links look
  right in LinkedIn and Slack.
- **Q6** Not download-only, and not a novel format that makes reviewers work
  (terminal or chat-only UIs).
- **Q7** Light and dark themes both correct.
